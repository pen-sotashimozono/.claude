# Citing

How the text points at a figure, a table or a source. In force in every
repository unless its `CLAUDE.md` lists this file under "Rules not in force".

The measure is a paper written by hand. A pointer is part of an ordinary
sentence, and neighbouring pointers are not all built the same way. A rule here
restricts what is written. None of them is met by adding a sentence.

- No figure or table in parentheses. Not "(Fig. 3)", not "(see Table 1)". The
  mention is a clause of a sentence.
- No panel and no position in the text. Not "Fig. 4(a)", "Fig. 4, left", "the
  top row of Fig. 5". Figures carry no panel letters. The caption says what the
  parts are, in the order they appear. If the text has to address one part
  alone, that part is a figure of its own.
- A figure or table is mentioned in one of two forms, and a section uses both.
  The float as subject: "Figure 3 shows both operations." The content as
  subject: "The diagrams of the three are shown in Fig. 1." Write "Figure" in
  full where it starts the sentence.
- A citation has two forms with different uses. To attribute a result, the bare
  citation follows the statement, or the authors are its subject: "… are
  standard results of linear algebra [2]", "Stewart traced the history of the
  SVD [4]". To send the reader somewhere, "in Ref. [5]": "Their use in tensor
  networks is reviewed in Ref. [5]". Do not write every citation of a paragraph
  as "in Ref. [n]".
- A phrasing that recurs is not a template. When three mentions or citations in
  a row have the same construction, rewrite one of them.
- A correction the author makes to one phrasing corrects that sentence. Do not
  answer it by writing the corrected form everywhere.
